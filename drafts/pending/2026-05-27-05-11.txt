90% of criminal cases now involve digital evidence. 70% of investigators say they don't have enough time to review it all.

That's not a technology problem. That's a capacity crisis - and AI is the only fix that scales.

1. Paperwork is consuming officer hours - Texas, Florida, and Washington are logging major time savings as AI auto-transcribes body cam footage and drafts incident reports. Hours back per officer, per shift.

2. Evidence backlog is the hidden bottleneck - A single case can demand 60+ hours of manual review. AI compresses that to minutes. DHS already runs 86 active AI use cases in law enforcement - more than any other domain in the agency.

3. Detection outcomes are measurably better - London's Metropolitan Police ran AI facial recognition pilots and saw a 19% increase in arrests. Chicago's predictive deployment models show real crime reduction in targeted areas.

4. Governance is the exposed flank - Federal AI policy is stalling while states scramble to fill the gap. Agencies deploying without clear accountability frameworks are accumulating risk they haven't fully priced in.

After 20+ years in cybersecurity - both offensive and defensive - I've seen this exact pattern: capability outrunning policy. It ends the same way every time. A high-profile failure forces the rules that should have been written first.

Who's accountable when an AI recommendation leads to a wrongful arrest - the vendor, the agency, or the officer who acted on it?
