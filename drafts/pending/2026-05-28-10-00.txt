86% of students already use AI in their studies. Only 10% of their schools have guidelines for it.

That's not a rollout delay. It's a structural gap that widens every semester.

What the 2026 data actually shows:

1. A peer-reviewed randomized controlled trial found AI tutoring outperformed traditional classroom instruction with an effect size of 0.73-1.3 standard deviations - among the strongest experimental results in modern education research.

2. Teachers using AI weekly save 5.9 hours. But 68% of urban teachers have received zero AI training. The productivity gain exists; the access to it doesn't.

3. AI cut IEP preparation time by 90% in special education settings. That's a workflow redesign, not a feature upgrade.

4. By 2030, 70% of job skills are expected to shift due to AI. The institutions responsible for preparing students for that world are mostly still catching up.

I teach AI and technology to high school students. The students aren't waiting for institutions - they're building their own workflows now. My job has shifted from teaching content to teaching judgment: when to use AI, when not to, and how to evaluate what it produces.

What's your school or organization actually doing to close that 10% guideline gap?
