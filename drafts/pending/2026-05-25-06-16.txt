92% of students now use AI as their primary research partner. Only 25% of their teachers feel trained to handle it.

That gap is not a technology problem. It's a training failure.

What the data shows in 2026:

1. Teachers using AI weekly reclaim 5.9 hours per week - 6 extra weeks per school year. Most of it flows back into admin, not better instruction.

2. 81% of educators lack time to build AI curriculum. 75% lack the knowledge to do it. Students have outpaced their teachers, and the gap keeps widening.

3. A peer-reviewed RCT showed AI tutoring beating classroom learning by 0.73-1.3 standard deviations - among the strongest results ever recorded for a learning intervention.

4. In special education, AI cut IEP prep time by 90%. One case: a teacher identified in one week - via AI pattern detection - that a student learned better through auditory than visual cues.

5. Teacher AI comfort doubled in one year, from 2.9 to 5.6 out of 10. Still below the halfway mark.

I teach AI to high school students. The kids are building workflows their teachers don't know exist.

The classroom is fine. The teacher training pipeline is not.

What happens when students know more about the tools than the people grading them?
